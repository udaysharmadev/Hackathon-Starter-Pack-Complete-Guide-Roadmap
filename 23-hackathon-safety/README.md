# Section 23: Hackathon Safety

You'd be surprised how many people forget this part. Hackathons are intense, exciting, and
all-consuming — but pushing your body and mind too hard can turn a fun weekend into a
nightmare. This section covers everything you need to know about staying healthy, sane, and
functional while you're grinding through 24-48 hours of building.

---

## 1. Burnout Prevention

Burnout doesn't happen suddenly. It creeps in. You've been staring at code for six hours,
you're on your third energy drink, and you keep telling yourself "just one more feature."
That's the trap.

**Recognizing the Signs:**
- You keep re-reading the same line of code without processing it
- Small bugs taking an hour instead of five minutes
- Irritability for no reason — a teammate's simple question makes you snap
- More typos than usual
- Second-guessing every architectural decision
- Food sounds disgusting but you know you need to eat

**The "One More Feature" Trap:**
This is the single biggest danger at a hackathon. You've got a working MVP, but then you
think "what if we added a chat feature?" Suddenly it's 3 AM, nothing works, and your team
is stressed. A clean demo of three working features beats a broken demo of seven
half-finished ones every single time.

**What to Do Instead:**
- Set hard feature boundaries by midnight on day one. Write them down.
- If you catch yourself saying "we could also..." after 2 AM, that's a no.
- Take a 15-minute break every 90 minutes.
- If someone on your team looks fried, tell them to take a break. Don't be a hero.

---

## 2. Screen Fatigue

Staring at screens for hours messes with your eyes, your sleep, and your ability to
think clearly.

**The 20-20-20 Rule:**
Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Set a timer on your
phone. It sounds too simple to work, but it genuinely helps reduce eye strain.

**Blue Light Management:**
- Use Night Shift on Mac or Night Light on Windows — crank it up in the evening
- Dark mode everywhere. Your eyes will thank you at 3 AM.
- Avoid bright overhead lights in a dark room — the contrast is brutal

**Eye Drops and Hydration:**
- Buy preservative-free eye drops (like Refresh or Systane) before the hackathon
- Dehydration makes eye strain worse. Keep water at your desk.
- If your eyes are burning, close them for 60 seconds. Don't power through.

---

## 3. Ergonomics

Most hackathon setups are terrible for your body. Folding chairs, low tables, laptops
at weird angles. Your neck and back will pay the price.

**Proper Laptop Position:**
- Top of screen at or slightly below eye level
- Prop laptop up on books or a stand, use an external keyboard
- Elbows at roughly 90 degrees when typing
- Wrists floating above the keyboard, not resting on the table edge

**Quick Desk Stretches:**
- Neck rolls: slowly roll head in circles, 10 each direction
- Shoulder shrugs: raise to ears, hold 5 seconds, release. Repeat 10 times.
- Wrist circles: extend arms, rotate wrists 10 each direction
- Seated spinal twist: turn torso left and right, hold 15 seconds each side
- Cat-cow: if you can get on the floor, do 10 reps. Great for your spine.

---

## 4. Nutrition During Hackathons

What you eat directly affects how well you think. That pizza and energy drink combo
might feel good in the moment, but you'll crash hard two hours later.

**Brain Foods:**
- Nuts and seeds — almonds, walnuts, pumpkin seeds for steady energy
- Bananas — natural sugars plus potassium
- Dark chocolate — a few squares, not the whole bar. Gentle caffeine boost.
- Whole grain crackers with peanut butter — sustained energy
- Hard-boiled eggs — protein without making you sluggish

**What to Avoid:**
- Energy drinks in excess. One is fine. Four is not.
- Sugary sodas and candy. Spike and crash at the worst time.
- Heavy pasta or pizza meals. They make you sleepy.
- Excessive caffeine after 4 PM if you want any chance of sleeping

**Hydration Schedule:**
Drink water before you're thirsty. Start your day with a full glass, keep a bottle
at your desk, and alternate coffee with water. If your urine is dark yellow, drink more.

---

## 5. Mental Health

Hackathons can be emotionally intense. You're working under pressure, sleep-deprived,
and comparing yourself to teams that seem to have it all figured out.

**Anxiety Management:**
- If overwhelmed, step away for 10 minutes. Breathe. Walk outside.
- Break work into tiny tasks. "Build the entire backend" is scary. "Set up one API
  endpoint" is doable.
- Talk to your teammates. They're probably feeling the same way.
- Remember: it's a hackathon, not a job interview.

**Imposter Syndrome:**
- Most teams are struggling with something, even if they don't show it
- The teams that look confident are usually just hiding their problems better
- Your project doesn't have to be the most technically impressive — it has to solve
  a real problem
- You were accepted into this hackathon for a reason. Trust that.

**Comparing Yourself to Other Teams:**
Don't do it. Every team has different skills and starting points. Judges evaluate based
on how well you solved your problem, not how many technologies you used.

---

## 6. Sleep Strategy

You can't go 48 hours without sleep and expect to perform well. Your brain needs rest.

**Power Naps:**
A 20-minute nap dramatically improves alertness. Set an alarm. Best time is 1-3 PM.
Don't nap after 4 PM if you want to sleep that night.

**The 90-Minute Cycle:**
If you can afford a longer sleep, aim for exactly 90 minutes — one full sleep cycle.
Waking up mid-cycle leaves you groggy. Waking up after a full cycle feels natural.

**Caffeine Timing:**
- First coffee when you naturally wake up, not the moment you roll out of bed
- Stop caffeine by 2-3 PM at the latest
- If you're tired at 11 PM, that's your body telling you to sleep — don't fight it

**When to Call It a Night:**
There's no shame in going to bed at midnight and waking up at 6 AM to finish. You'll
produce better work well-rested than exhausted. The code will still be there in the morning.

## 7. Physical Activity

Movement isn't optional during a hackathon — it's necessary. Your brain needs blood
flow, your muscles need relief, and your mood needs a boost.

**Stretching Routine (every 90 minutes):**
1. Neck rolls — 10 in each direction, slow and controlled
2. Shoulder rolls — 10 forward, 10 backward
3. Chest opener — clasp hands behind back, lift gently, hold 15 seconds
4. Standing forward fold — hang loose, let gravity stretch your hamstrings
5. Hip circles — hands on hips, 10 circles each direction
6. Calf raises — 20 reps, helps blood flow from sitting
7. Wrist stretches — extend arm, pull fingers back gently, 15 seconds each side

**Walking Breaks:**
Walk for at least 5 minutes every hour. If the venue has outdoor space, use it. Some
teams make it a rule: if you're stuck on a problem, take a walk. Movement helps your
brain process in the background.

---

## 8. Emergency Contacts

Things go wrong. People get sick, have allergic reactions, or feel faint from
dehydration. Know what to do before it happens.

**If Someone Feels Unwell:**
1. Stay calm and assess the situation
2. Ask what's wrong — headache, nausea, chest pain, dizziness?
3. For dehydration or faintness: sit them down, give water, get something sugary
4. For chest pain or difficulty breathing: call emergency services immediately
5. Let the organizers know. They have protocols for this.

**What to Find Out Before the Hackathon:**
- Where the nearest hospital or urgent care is
- Whether the venue has a first aid kit and where it's located
- Who the event organizers are and how to contact them
- Whether the venue has AED equipment

**Emergency Kit to Bring:**
- Any personal medications you need
- Pain relievers (ibuprofen, acetaminophen)
- Antihistamines for allergic reactions
- A copy of your insurance card or photo on your phone

## 9. "When to Quit"

Not every hackathon project is worth finishing. Sometimes the smartest move is
cutting your losses.

**Signs a Project Isn't Worth Continuing:**
- Stuck on the same fundamental problem for 6+ hours with no progress
- Your core premise has a flaw you can't work around
- Nobody on the team understands the problem anymore
- You're building something that already exists with no clear differentiator
- Your team has completely run out of motivation

**How to Exit Gracefully:**
- If pivoting, do it early — not at hour 20
- Be honest: "I'm burned out and I can't contribute meaningfully right now"
- Don't ghost your team. Tell them directly.
- You can still attend the closing ceremony and network even if you didn't finish

**Pivoting vs. Quitting:**
Sometimes you don't need to quit — you need to simplify dramatically. If your original
idea was a full-stack AI platform, maybe you pivot to a simple web app that demonstrates
the core concept. That's still worth presenting.

---

## 10. Post-Hackathon Recovery

The hackathon ends and you crash. Here's what to do in the first 24 hours.

**The First Evening:**
- Eat a real, balanced meal
- Drink plenty of water
- Sleep if you can. Don't stay up scrolling your phone.
- Avoid alcohol — your body is already stressed and dehydrated

**The Next Day:**
- Sleep in if you can
- Go outside. Get sunlight. Walk around.
- Don't open your laptop for at least a few hours
- Light exercise — a walk, stretching, yoga
- Reflect on the experience: what went well, what you'd do differently

**Warning Signs to Watch For:**
If these persist for more than a few days, talk to someone:
- Persistent exhaustion that doesn't improve with sleep
- Difficulty concentrating on normal tasks
- Feeling detached or unmotivated
- Eye problems that don't resolve

---

## Final Thought

The hackathon is supposed to be fun. If it stops being fun, something needs to change.
Take care of yourself, take care of your team, and remember: there's always another
hackathon. Your health isn't worth sacrificing for a weekend project.
