# Pitch Script Templates

Three pitch lengths, filled out with examples. Pick the one that matches your hackathon's time limit. Every template uses the same emotional arc: grab attention → show pain → reveal solution → prove it works → paint the future → close strong.

---

## 30-Second Elevator Pitch

Use this when judges rotate between tables or you get stopped in a hallway. Speed matters, but clarity matters more.

**Timing target: 28-32 seconds (practice with a stopwatch)**

```
[Make eye contact, stand tall]

"Ever spent 20 minutes looking for a parking spot on campus? Yeah, us too."

[Pause 1 second]

"We built ParkFinder — an app that shows real-time parking availability
using camera feeds from existing security cams. No new hardware, no
construction. Just open the app, see green spots, drive there."

[Gesture toward phone/screen]

"We tested it at UC Davis last week. Average search time dropped from
14 minutes to 3. That's 11 minutes back in every student's day."

[Step forward slightly]

"We're [Name] from [Team], and we'd love to show you how it works."

[Pause for response — this is your demo segue]
```

**Emotional arc:** Relatable frustration → surprising simplicity → concrete proof → invitation

## 60-Second Standard Pitch

This is your bread and butter. Most hackathons give 2-3 minutes, but you'll use the first 60 seconds to hook them. Even if you get more time, nail this version first.

**Timing target: 55-65 seconds**

```
[Stand center, don't hide behind the table]

"Hi, I'm [Name], and this is [Team]. Let me ask you something—"

[Pause. Look at the judges.]

"How many of you checked your email this morning before getting out of
bed? Probably all of you. Now how many of you felt焦虑 about the
unread count? That's the problem we're solving."

[Step to screen/laptop]

"Meet InboxZero AI. It's a Chrome extension that reads your emails and
auto-categorizes them into three buckets: needs action today, can wait,
and newsletter/promo. But here's the twist—it learns from YOUR
decisions. Mark something wrong once, it never makes that mistake again."

[Click to demo screen. Show the extension working.]

"We built this in 12 hours with Next.js, OpenAI's API, and a Chrome
extension framework. In our test with 50 users, average email processing
time went from 23 minutes to 4 minutes per day."

[Turn back to judges]

"That's 15 minutes saved daily. Over a year, that's 91 hours —
basically two work weeks, back in your life."

[Pause]

"We're seeking seed funding—just kidding. But we ARE seeking your
feedback. Questions?"

[Smile. Relax. You nailed it.]
```

**Emotional arc:** Universal pain → product reveal → proof of work → real-world impact → confident close

## 3-Minute Deep Dive Pitch

Use this when judges specifically ask for a walkthrough, or when the hackathon gives you 3+ minutes. This version adds technical depth and a demo flow.

**Timing target: 2:45-3:15**

```
== PART 1: THE HOOK (0:00-0:30) ==

[Start with a story, not a slide]

"Last November, my roommate Sarah almost missed her flight because
she couldn't find her passport. She tore apart our apartment for
45 minutes. Found it in a textbook she'd never opened."

[Pause]

"Turns out, 67% of people lose at least one important document per
year. That's 220 million hours wasted just searching for stuff you
already own."

== PART 2: THE SOLUTION (0:30-1:15) ==

[Walk toward your setup]

"We built DocuVault. It's a mobile app that lets you photograph any
document — passport, insurance card, lease, warranty — and stores it
with AI-powered tagging and OCR."

[Show phone screen or demo]

"Watch this. I photograph my passport. In 3 seconds, the app extracts
my name, passport number, expiration date, and auto-tags it as
'government ID - travel.' It's encrypted end-to-end and stored locally
first, cloud-synced optionally."

[Gesture to architecture diagram]

"Under the hood: React Native for cross-platform, Tesseract.js for
OCR, AES-256 encryption, and Firebase for optional cloud sync.
Everything runs on-device by default — your data never touches our
servers unless you choose to."

== PART 3: THE DEMO (1:15-2:00) ==

[Do a LIVE demo here — not screenshots]

"Let me show you the full workflow. I'm going to:
1. Photograph this piece of paper [hold up a prop]
2. Watch it auto-extract and tag
3. Search for it using natural language: 'What's my account number for...?'
4. Show you the encrypted export feature"

[Run through demo. Narrate each step. If something fails, see the
emergency script at the bottom of this file.]

== PART 4: THE PROOF (2:00-2:30) ==

[Turn to slide or screen showing metrics]

"We tested with 30 users over 2 weeks:
- Average document retrieval time: 2 seconds (down from 8 minutes)
- 94% said they'd use it weekly
- Zero security incidents in testing

We also submitted to the App Store yesterday — pending review."

== PART 5: THE CLOSE (2:30-3:00) ==

[Step forward, make eye contact with each judge]

"DocuVault solves a problem everyone has but nobody talks about.
We're [Name], [Name], and [Name]. We built this in 36 hours,
and we think it could genuinely help millions of people."

[Pause 2 seconds — let it land]

"We'd love to answer your questions. And if you want to try it,
here's the TestFlight link."

[Hand out cards or show QR code]
```

**Emotional arc:** Personal story → scale of problem → solution reveal → live proof → social validation → confident future

## Emergency Script: When Your Demo Breaks

Memorize this. Print it on an index card. Keep it in your pocket. Every demo fails at least once.

```
[If the demo crashes or freezes]

"No worries — technology keeps us humble. Let me walk you through
what just happened."

[Pull up pre-recorded video, screenshots, or a backup device]

"Here's a 30-second clip of it working perfectly earlier today.
I'll walk you through the same flow."

[If video also fails]

"Fair enough — the demo gods are testing us. Let me show you the
screenshots and architecture instead. The key point is..."

[Transition to your static backup — printed screenshots, a
diagram, or a whiteboard sketch]

[If NOTHING works]

"Honestly, this is a great sign — it means our infrastructure is
so secure even WE can't break into it right now."

[Light laugh, then pivot]

"But seriously, here's what we built and why it matters. The code
is on GitHub, the live demo is at [URL], and I'm happy to do a
one-on-one walkthrough after judging. Can I tell you about the
technical architecture instead?"

[Always have ready]
- Pre-recorded demo video (30-60 sec, uploaded to YouTube)
- 5+ screenshots saved locally (not just in the cloud)
- A printed one-page summary you can hand to judges
- A backup device (teammate's phone/laptop) with the app running
```

## Emotional Arc Map

Every great pitch follows this shape:

```
Engagement
    ^
    |     /\
    |    /  \         /\
    |   /    \       /  \
    |  /      \     /    \
    | /        \   /      \
    |/    A     \ /   C    \
    +-----|------|-----|-------> Time
          B      D     E

A = Peak of problem statement (frustration, urgency)
B = Valley of "here's the pain" (empathy moment)
C = Peak of solution reveal (hope, excitement)
D = Valley of "but does it work?" (demo, proof)
E = Peak of closing (confidence, future vision)
```

**Rules:**
- Never start flat. Always open with energy or a question.
- The problem section should feel heavier than the solution. Make them feel the pain first.
- The demo is where tension peaks. Live demos are inherently risky — that tension is your friend.
- End on a high. Never trail off. End with a clear ask or confident statement.

## Stage Directions Key

| Direction | When to Use |
|-----------|-------------|
| `[pause]` | After a surprising fact, before a reveal, or after a question. Silence is powerful. |
| `[gesture to screen]` | When transitioning from talking to showing. Guide their eyes. |
| `[step forward]` | When you want intimacy or emphasis. Move toward the judges. |
| `[step back]` | When giving space for the audience to absorb something big. |
| `[make eye contact]` | During the opening and closing. Never stare at your notes. |
| `[smile]` | After the close. After a joke. When the demo works. Humans trust smiles. |
| `[hold up prop]` | Physical objects grab attention. Passports, phones, a crumpled paper. |

## Practice Checklist

- [ ] Timed each version 3+ times (aim for the middle of the range, not the max)
- [ ] Practiced with someone watching (not just in the mirror)
- [ ] Recorded yourself on video and watched it back
- [ ] Rehearsed the emergency script 2+ times until it feels natural
- [ ] Printed backup slides/screenshots
- [ ] Tested demo on the exact device you'll use at the venue
- [ ] Practiced transitions between sections (these are where most stumbles happen)
- [ ] Prepared 2-3 answers for likely judge questions
