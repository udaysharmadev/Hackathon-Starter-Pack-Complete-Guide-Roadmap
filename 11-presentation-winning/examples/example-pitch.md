# Example 60-Second Pitch: TrackIntern

**Project:** TrackIntern — an internship application tracker for college students
**Context:** 200-person hackathon, 3-person team, judging panel of 5 (2 VCs, 2 engineering managers, 1 professor)
**Goal:** Win "Best Overall" by making judges feel the problem, see the solution, and remember the impact

---

## The Full Pitch Script with Timing

**[0:00–0:08] THE HOOK — Make them feel the problem**

> "Sixty-seven percent of college students miss at least one internship deadline each year. Not because they're lazy — because they're tracking applications in spreadsheets, sticky notes, and browser tabs that they forgot about."

🎤 *Voice: Serious, slightly slower pace. Let the number land. Pause after "each year" for 1 second. Emphasize "forgot about" — that's the emotional trigger.*

📝 *Judge's perspective: The 67% statistic is specific and surprising. It creates urgency. The "not because they're lazy" line pre-empts the judge's mental objection ("why don't they just use a calendar?"). You're showing empathy for the user.*

---

**[0:08–0:15] THE BRIDGE — Connect problem to your solution**

> "I was one of those students. Last semester, I missed a Google application deadline by two hours because I had the date written on a Post-it note that fell off my wall. That's when I knew there had to be a better way."

🎤 *Voice: Personal, conversational. Drop the formal tone. This is your story. Make eye contact with one judge.*

📝 *Judge's perspective: Personal stories are memorable. Judges hear 30 pitches — the ones with real stories stick. "Two hours" is painfully specific. "Post-it note that fell off my wall" is visual and relatable. Every judge has lost a Post-it.*

---

**[0:15–0:30] THE DEMO — Show, don't tell**

> "So we built TrackIntern."

*(Click to live demo. Login with pre-filled credentials. Dashboard loads.)*

> "Here's what a student sees when they log in. Four cards at the top — applied, interview, offer, rejected — so you know exactly where you stand. Every internship is listed below, sorted by the nearest deadline."

*(Scroll down the dashboard. Hover over a status badge.)*

> "Color-coded badges mean you can scan 20 applications in two seconds. Green means offer. Yellow means interview pending. Blue means applied. Red means rejected — and we don't judge you for those."

*(Click "Add Internship." Fill in the form quickly.)*

> "Adding an internship takes ten seconds. Company, role, deadline, status. That's it. No 40-field form. No onboarding flow. Just... add it and go."

*(Submit the form. Toast notification appears. Dashboard updates.)*

> "And three days before each deadline, you get a reminder. No more Post-it notes."

🎤 *Voice: Confident but not salesy. Let the demo do the talking. Narrate actions simply — "here's what you see" not "our algorithm processes." End the demo with a slight smile on "No more Post-it notes."*

📝 *Judge's perspective: The demo is fast (15 seconds) and shows the core loop: see → add → get reminded. The "no judgment" line adds personality. Judges are watching your UI — the color-coded badges and clean layout are doing the selling. The toast notification is a small polish detail that signals quality.*

---

**[0:30–0:42] THE SCALE — Show this isn't a toy**

> "We built this in six hours with Next.js, Tailwind CSS, and PostgreSQL. It's deployed on Vercel, connected to a real database, and ready to handle thousands of users. In our user testing with 30 students on campus, 87% said they'd use this over their current method."

🎤 *Voice: Faster pace here. You're stacking credibility. Numbers create authority. "Real database" and "ready for thousands" signal this isn't a weekend prototype.*

📝 *Judge's perspective: Technical credibility matters. Mentioning the stack briefly shows you know what you're doing. The "30 students" testing claim is concrete — judges are suspicious of "we tested with users" without numbers. "87%" is specific enough to be believable but not so high it sounds fake.*

---

**[0:42–0:52] THE VISION — Where does this go?**

> "But tracking is just the start. We're building an AI engine that analyzes your application history and tells you which companies are most likely to respond, based on your major, GPA, and past success patterns. We're turning a scattered spreadsheet into a personal career strategist."

🎤 *Voice: Slower, more deliberate. Paint the vision. This is the "dream big" moment. Lean forward slightly — you're sharing something exciting.*

📝 *Judge's perspective: The vision matters because judges are investing in a team, not just a project. "AI engine that analyzes your application history" is specific enough to be credible but ambitious enough to be exciting. You're not saying "we'll add AI" — you're describing exactly what the AI does. The "personal career strategist" metaphor makes it memorable.*

---

**[0:52–0:60] THE CLOSE — Leave them wanting more**

> "We're TrackIntern. Sixty-seven percent of students miss deadlines because the tools aren't built for them. We're changing that. Thank you."

🎤 *Voice: Confident, measured. Don't rush the close. "We're TrackIntern" — pause — then deliver the line. "Thank you" with a nod. Then stop talking. Don't fill the silence.*

📝 *Judge's perspective: The callback to the opening statistic creates a satisfying narrative loop. You started with the problem, you're ending with the solution. "We're changing that" is bold but not arrogant — it's a commitment. The silence after "thank you" shows confidence. Nervous presenters ramble. You stopped.*

## Post-Pitch: Judge Q&A Prep

Anticipated questions and our answers:

**Q: "How is this different from spreadsheets?"**
A: "Spreadsheets require you to remember to open them. TrackIntern comes to you. The reminder system means the tool does the remembering, not the student."

**Q: "What's your business model?"**
A: "Free for students forever. We partner with career centers who pay $5 per student per semester for analytics on their advising effectiveness. The students get a free tool, the university gets data, we get revenue."

**Q: "How do you get users?"**
A: "We already have 30 from our campus testing. Career centers have 500-2,000 students each. One partnership gives us 500 users on day one. We're targeting 5 campus partnerships in the first semester."

## Why This Pitch Works: Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown

| Sentence | Technique | Why It Works |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| "67% of students miss deadlines" | Specific statistic | Creates immediate credibility and urgency |
| "Not because they're lazy" | Pre-empts objection | Shows you understand the nuance |
| "I was one of those students" | Personal story | Makes you relatable, not just a presenter |
| "Post-it note that fell off my wall" | Visual detail | Judges can see it happening to them |
| "Color-coded badges mean you can scan 20 in two seconds" | Quantified benefit | Makes the value tangible |
| "No judgment" (about red badges) | Humor | Breaks tension, shows personality |
| "Ready for thousands of users" | Scale signal | Tells judges this is investment-worthy |
| "87% said they'd use this" | User validation | Third-party proof beats self-promotion |
| "Personal career strategist" | Metaphor | Makes the vision sticky and memorable |
| "We're changing that" | Bold close | Shows conviction without arrogance |

## Timing Cheat Sheet

| Segment | Time | Cumulative |
|---------|------|------------|
| Hook (problem) | 8 sec | 0:08 |
| Bridge (personal story) | 7 sec | 0:15 |
| Demo (live walkthrough) | 15 sec | 0:30 |
| Scale (tech + traction) | 12 sec | 0:42 |
| Vision (future) | 10 sec | 0:52 |
| Close (callback + name) | 8 sec | 1:00 |

**Total: 60 seconds exactly.**

Practice with a timer. If you're over 60 seconds, cut the vision section by 3 seconds and the demo narration by 2 seconds. Never cut the hook or the close — those are non-negotiable.
